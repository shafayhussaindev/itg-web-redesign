# ITG website: company content request and complete Tier 2 page inventory

Prepared from the current website source on 21 September 2026.

## Scope and how to use this document

This covers the 27 Tier 2 pages discussed in our redesign: 5 solution pages, 5 product-category pages, 6 service-category pages and 11 industry pages. Their menus contain 112 nested entries: 31 solution capabilities, 10 products, 20 services and 51 industry segments. These are sections within the 27 pages, not 112 separate Tier 3 pages.

The names and offerings below are the current website inventory, not independently verified company facts. Ask the company to mark every entry: **confirmed / needs correction / planned / do not publish**. A page in the codebase does not establish that ITG has delivered that offering. Existing draft claims and examples need a company reviewer.

Send the shared request once, then assign each page to the appropriate business owner. The company can supply bullet points, brochures and source material; polished website prose is not required. Suggested word counts below are editorial targets, not rigid software limits.

## 1. Copy-and-send request to the company

> We are preparing the final content for ITG's website. Please appoint a content coordinator and technical/business reviewers for Solutions, Products, Services and Industries.
>
> First, confirm the attached list of 27 pages and their offerings. Mark each item as confirmed, needing correction, planned or not for publication.
>
> For each confirmed page, please provide the target customer, their main problems, what ITG delivers, three supported benefits, relevant products/technologies, available evidence, approved imagery and the appropriate enquiry contact. For each product, also provide its current features, availability, screenshots and demo process.
>
> Please share existing company profiles, brochures, sales decks, product sheets, approved case studies, partner/certification details, brand files and usable photos. For customer names, logos, quotes and results, indicate what may be published.
>
> Please identify a source and reviewer for factual claims. If information is unavailable, write “not available” or “pending confirmation.” Mark confidential background material separately from publishable copy.
>
> We will turn the supplied facts into website copy and return it for review. Please nominate one final approver and an agreed review date.

Do not assume an office photo, client logo, partner badge or stock photo proves a delivered project. Label illustrative imagery accurately.

## 3. Minimum content package for every page

Use this form for each of the 27 pages:

- **Page name and URL:**
- **Status:** confirmed / needs correction / planned / do not publish
- **Business owner and reviewer:**
- **Primary audience:** role, organization type, geography and size if relevant
- **Main customer problem:** three specific issues in the customer's own terms
- **What ITG delivers:** scope and boundaries, in plain language
- **Three benefits:** expected benefits or measured results, clearly distinguished
- **Relevant offering details:** complete the family template below
- **Differentiators:** two or three facts that explain ITG's approach
- **Proof/source:** approved project, product documentation, reference or named subject-matter expert
- **Imagery:** file/link, description, owner and publication approval
- **Next action:** demo / consultation / expert discussion, with recipient or destination
- **SEO brief:** target audience/search intent, suggested page title and summary
- **Approval status, reviewer and date:**

For every numerical result, collect the baseline, resulting value, measurement period, project context and source. “Reduces processing time” is an intended benefit; “reduced processing time by 35%” requires a documented result and permission to publish it.

## 4. Content needed by page family

### A. Solution pages: customer problem → capabilities → business outcomes

Current structure: hero; section navigation; overview with three outcomes; capability guide; three application examples; image/feature section; four-step approach; related solutions; closing contact section.

| Current section | Ask the company for | Suggested amount |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | Core promise, who it serves, short explanation and three themes | Headline 8–14 words; summary 35–55 words; three short tags |
| Overview | Business problem, operational context and ITG's response | Heading plus 100–150 words total |
| Outcomes | Three concrete benefits with scope/qualification | Three titles and 20–35 words each |
| Each capability | Name, short purpose, explanation, delivery focus and enabled outcomes | Subtitle; 45–75 words; three focus bullets; two outcome bullets |
| Applications | Three realistic situations where the solution applies | Title and 30–45 words each; identify illustrative versus delivered |
| Feature story | Distinctive delivery principle or substantiated project story, supporting image and three points | Title; 60–100 words; three points |
| Approach | Confirm Discover, Design, Deliver, Evolve or supply the actual method | Shared across the five solution pages |
| Contact | Specific next step and correct enquiry destination | Heading, 25–40 words and button action |

Ask for the underlying problem, capabilities and expected result. A solutions page should not merely repeat a service list or product brochure.

### B. Product pages: what is available → what it does → how to evaluate it

Current structure: category hero with product selector; category overview; product catalogue; integration/governance/expansion section; related categories; closing contact section.

| Current section | Ask the company for | Suggested amount |
|---|---|---|
| Category hero | Category purpose, audience, summary and three themes | Headline; 35–55 words; three tags |
| Overview | Why the products belong together and when a buyer should consider them | Lead sentence and 80–120 words |
| Each product card | Correct name, short description, product explanation and three principal functions | Description 8–15 words; body 45–75 words; three focus bullets |
| Integration section | Actual system connections, deployment considerations, data governance and expansion path | Shared category-level explanation plus three points |
| Contact | Demo or enquiry process, recipient and booking/demo link if available | Actual destination and approved CTA |

**Additional product facts to collect even if not all are shown in the current cards:**

1. Ownership: proprietary ITG product, licensed third-party product, reseller offering or implementation service.
2. Status: generally available, limited release, pilot or planned; current version if meaningful to buyers.
3. Users and use cases: buyer role, daily users, sectors and problems solved.
4. Features: available modules, optional modules, supported workflows and product limitations.
5. Deployment: SaaS, private cloud, on-premises or hybrid only where supported.
6. Integrations: name each supported system and identify native connectors versus bespoke integration work.
7. Security/administration: roles, authentication, audit functions, data location and retention as actually supported.
8. Adoption: onboarding, migration, configuration, training and support.
9. Commercial information: licensing/pricing model and whether it should be public or quotation-only.
10. Evidence/assets: sanitized screenshots, walkthroughs, product sheets, sample outputs and approved customer examples.

The current templates do **not** include dedicated screenshot galleries, pricing tables, downloads or demo forms. Collecting those assets is useful; publishing them in new sections would require a further layout/content addition. Never present conceptual UI as an actual product screenshot.
